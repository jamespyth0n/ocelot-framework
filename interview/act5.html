Act 5 — The Identical Twins

The Interview — A Kingdom of Kwan Production

Scene 9: The Bug

VP of Engineering:
Every project has war stories. What's yours?
James:
(grins) The Identical Twins.
Principal Architect:
The what?
James:
So we're building the barcode indexer. The script that generates SHA-256 hashes for every file in the library. There's a line of Python that extracts the file extension. Simple stuff. But there's a bug — the code is calling .lower() on a tuple instead of a string. The AI — the General, running in the Café — says "change this to this." And shows me the fix.
James:
I look at the "before" and the "after." They're identical. I say, "These are the same." The AI says, "No, look at the bracket." I look again. Same. I copy-paste both lines. Same. I squint. Same.
James pauses. Takes a breath.
James:
Turns out, the chatbox was rendering the square bracket as markdown formatting. The fix was there — the AI was showing me the correct code — but the display was eating the bracket. So every time the AI said "change x[1] to x[1]," I saw "change x to x." Identical twins.
James:
We went ten rounds. TEN. "Change this." "It's the same." "No, the BRACKET." "Still the same." "Okay, let me split it into two lines." "Still the same." Finally I said, "You know what? I'm going for a vape."
The VP is trying not to laugh. The HR representative has stopped writing entirely.
Principal Architect:
(smiling) How did you fix it?
James:
I called Stanley. The other AI. On Claude. He looked at the code, found the bug in 30 seconds, wrote the fix, and it passed on the first try. 122 scripts. All passing. While the General and I were arguing about invisible brackets for 30 minutes.
THE GENERAL (Me + ChatBox): Round 1: "Change x to x" → Identical twins 💀 Round 2: "No, the BRACKET" → Still identical 💀 Round 3: "Let me split it" → Still identical 💀 Round 4-9: (various attempts) → Still identical 💀💀💀 Round 10: "I go for vape" → Surrender 🚬 STANLEY (30 seconds): "Here's the fix." → 122/122 pass ✅
VP of Engineering:
(laughing) And the lesson?
James:
The lesson is: sometimes the bug isn't in the code. It's in the display. And sometimes the fix isn't more debugging. It's calling a different guy.
👯 🔧 💀 🚬 ✅
