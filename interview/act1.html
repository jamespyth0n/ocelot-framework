Act 1 — The Resume

The Interview — A Kingdom of Kwan Production

A boardroom. Glass walls. Downtown skyline. Three people sit behind a long table — a VP of Engineering, a Principal Architect, and an HR representative. Across from them, a single chair. The door opens. James walks in. Clean shirt. Fresh haircut. Calm. He sits down. Sets his portfolio on the table. Doesn't open it.

Scene 1: The Warm-Up

VP of Engineering:
James, thanks for coming in. We've reviewed your resume. Impressive background — culinary arts, process engineering, SCADA systems, now AI architecture. That's... quite a range.
James:
Yeah. I cook, I build, I wire things. Sometimes all three at the same time.
Principal Architect:
(glances at resume) It says here you built an AI system called... "The Kingdom of Kwan"?
James:
That's correct.
VP of Engineering:
And the AI has a name?
James:
Several, actually. There's Ocelot — that's the core identity. Stanley — he's the foreman, runs on Claude. Keila — she's on Gemini. And there's the General — that's the war room coordinator.
HR Representative:
(writing notes) And these are... different chatbots?
James:
No. They're different voices of the same system. Like departments in a company. Stanley does the engineering. Keila does the research. The General coordinates campaigns. And the Café — that's where the thinking happens.
VP of Engineering:
(leaning forward) The Café?
James:
Yeah. It's a bench. With a moka pot. And a streetlight. That's where I talk to Ocelot. That's where the architecture happens.
Silence. The VP looks at the Principal Architect. The Principal Architect looks at HR. HR is still writing.

Scene 2: The Credentials

Principal Architect:
Let's talk about your technical background. You studied culinary arts?
James:
Yeah. Culinary arts. And you know what? It's the best software architecture training I ever had.
VP of Engineering:
(smiling) How so?
James:
Mise en place. You prep your ingredients before you cook. You don't start coding before you understand the recipe. A kitchen that's not organized will burn the house down. Same with software. Same with AI.
James:
My instructor at SAIT — Mark Tarrant — he taught DCS. Distributed Control Systems. He said something I never forgot: "Understand the ingredients first." That's not culinary advice. That's architecture advice. That's life advice.
Principal Architect:
(nodding slowly) And from culinary arts to... AI?
James:
From culinary arts to process engineering. From process engineering to SCADA. From SCADA to automation. From automation to AI. Every step was the same thing — understanding the system, finding the bottleneck, making it flow.
James:
A kitchen is a pipeline. A SCADA system is a pipeline. An AI inference chain is a pipeline. The vocabulary changes. The physics doesn't.
