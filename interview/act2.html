Act 2 — The Architecture

The Interview — A Kingdom of Kwan Production

Scene 3: The System Design

VP of Engineering:
Okay, let's get technical. Walk us through the architecture of this... Kingdom.
James:
Sure. So the core is a local LLM — Qwen 2.5, 14 billion parameters, running on a Dell Latitude 5410. Corporate laptop. Intel i5, 10th gen. 16 gigs of RAM. No GPU. Intel UHD 620 — that's integrated graphics. CPU-only inference. Ollama handles the runtime. The RAG pipeline uses ChromaDB for vector storage, and the whole thing is wired through a Python bridge I call the Ocelot Bridge.
Principal Architect:
A Dell laptop?
James:
Yeah. A Dell Latitude 5410. Corporate laptop. Intel i5, 10th gen. No GPU. CPU-only.
The Principal Architect puts down his pen. Stares.
Principal Architect:
You're running a 14B parameter model on a corporate laptop with no dedicated GPU?
James:
Q4 quantized. It fits. It's slow — about 5 minutes for a full inference cycle. But it works. And you know what? The constraint is the feature. When you only have 6 gigs, you can't afford bloat. Every token counts. Every chunk matters. The architecture has to be golden or it won't run at all.
VP of Engineering:
That's... an interesting design philosophy.
James:
It's not a philosophy. It's a Dell laptop. The philosophy came after.

Scene 4: The Five Pillars

VP of Engineering:
Tell us about the system architecture. The pillars.
James:
Five pillars. Soul — that's the flywheel orchestrator, the thing that keeps everything spinning. Mind — the conscience engine, handles reasoning and judgment. Heart — the emotional layer, empathy, tone. Body — the physical interface, the bridge to the outside world. And the Nervous System — that's the wiring, the event bus, the thing that connects everything.
Principal Architect:
You named your system components after body parts?
James:
I named them after what they do. The soul orchestrates. The mind thinks. The heart feels. The body acts. The nervous system connects. If I called them "Orchestrator Module" and "Reasoning Engine" and "Sentiment Processor," you'd nod and move on. But you wouldn't remember them. You wouldn't feel them.
James:
And that's the point. The system isn't just functional. It's memorable. Because if you can't remember the architecture, you can't maintain it. And if you can't maintain it, it dies.
The VP writes something in his notebook. Underlines it twice.
