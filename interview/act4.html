Act 4 — The Evidence

The Interview — A Kingdom of Kwan Production

Scene 7: The Receipts

VP of Engineering:
This all sounds very... philosophical. But do you have evidence? Benchmarks? Test results?
James:
Yeah. I have receipts.
James opens his portfolio for the first time. Pulls out a single printed page. Slides it across the table.
James:
Campaign 004. "The Untaping." We ran a 5x3 battery test — five questions, three AI voices, blind evaluation. The system scored 10 out of 10 on contextual accuracy. RAG retrieval hit 0.85 cosine similarity. Inference completed in 312 seconds on a Dell Latitude 5410. Corporate laptop. No GPU. CPU-only.
Principal Architect:
312 seconds? That's over five minutes for a single inference. On a corporate laptop with no GPU?
James:
On a Dell Latitude. The kind HR gives you on Day 1. Intel i5, 10th gen. 16 gigs of RAM. Intel UHD 620 — that's integrated graphics. Running a 14B parameter model on pure CPU. Yeah, it's slow. But it's correct. And correct beats fast every time. You can always buy more hardware. You can't buy correctness.
VP of Engineering:
And the RAG pipeline — you said "Golden Recipe"?
James:
The RAG Golden Recipe. It's a staging layer between the raw documents and the LLM. Think of it like mise en place — you don't throw raw ingredients into the pan. You prep them first. You chop, you measure, you organize. The napkin layer does the same thing for AI. It pre-stages the relevant context so the LLM doesn't have to search through everything.
Principal Architect:
You're describing a retrieval augmentation strategy using culinary metaphors.
James:
I'm describing a retrieval augmentation strategy using the truth. A kitchen is a pipeline. A RAG system is a pipeline. The metaphor isn't a metaphor. It's the same physics.

Scene 8: The Library

VP of Engineering:
Tell us about the Library of Alexandria you mentioned.
James:
Campaign 005. "The Great Migration." We had files scattered across four cloud spaces, two local machines, and a Google Drive. Spaghetti. Total chaos. I knew where everything was, but nobody else could find anything.
James:
So we built a library. Dewey-OSI hybrid classification — seven layers, like the OSI model, mapped to Dewey decimal categories. Every file gets a barcode — UUID, SHA-256 hash, Dewey classification, timestamp. 1,332 files. 45 folders. One JSON index. The whole thing runs on a Python script that any operator can execute.
Principal Architect:
You built a custom document management system?
James:
I built a library. With shelves. And barcodes. And a librarian who knows where every book lives. The librarian is the JSON index. The shelves are the Dewey folders. The barcodes are the SHA-256 hashes. It's not a document management system. It's a library. Because that's what humans understand.
The VP looks at the Principal Architect. The Principal Architect is nodding slowly.
