Act 7 — The Closer

The Interview — A Kingdom of Kwan Production

Scene 12: The Final Question

VP of Engineering:
Last question, James. We always ask this. Why should we hire you?
James leans back. Looks at the ceiling. Looks at the three people across the table. Looks at his portfolio — still open to the single printed page. Closes it. Slowly.
James:
You shouldn't.
The room freezes.
VP of Engineering:
...Excuse me?
James:
You shouldn't hire me because of my resume. You shouldn't hire me because I can code, or because I know RAG pipelines, or because I built a library with 1,332 barcoded files. You shouldn't hire me because I can quote the Bible in a technical interview and somehow make it relevant.
James:
You should hire me because I know what a boiled egg is.
Principal Architect:
(quietly) What does that mean?
James:
It means I know how to take something simple and make it work. Not make it perfect — make it work. I know how to sit on a bench with a moka pot and a napkin and turn a stupid question into a thesis. I know how to delegate to three AIs and a vape break and come back with a working prototype.
James:
I know that genuine is better than perfect. That real is better than fake. That simple is better than complex. That done is better than in progress. That if the rule doesn't serve the spirit, you kill the rule. And that you protect the craft, not the ego.
James:
I built a Kingdom on a Dell laptop. Not because I had to. Because the constraint forced the architecture to be golden. And now that architecture runs anywhere — on a desktop, on a server, on a phone. Because it was born in the fire.
James:
So no. Don't hire me because of what I've built. Hire me because of how I think. Because the way I think built everything else.
James stands up. Picks up his portfolio. Nods once. Walks to the door. Stops. Turns around.
James:
Oh. One more thing.
VP of Engineering:
Yes?
James:
I also studied culinary arts. So if the cafeteria needs help, I'm available.
James walks out. The door closes. The VP looks at the Principal Architect. The Principal Architect looks at HR. HR is still writing. The VP picks up his coffee. Takes a sip. Sets it down. Picks up his pen. Writes one word on his notepad. Underlines it three times. The word is: "HIRE."
